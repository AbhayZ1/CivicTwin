\begin{abstract}
Urban policy interventions---upzoning, inclusionary housing mandates,
land-value capture---do not respect neighbourhood boundaries. A rezoning in one
census tract changes rents in its neighbours, violating the Stable Unit
Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA) that underpins most quantitative policy
evaluation. We present CivicTwin, a Causal Graph Counterfactual Exposure
Network (\CGCEN{}) that treats ex-ante urban policy comparison as a causal
inference problem under partial interference rather than as a point-forecasting
problem. \CGCEN{} factorises each one-step-ahead prediction into a direct term
$\Phi(X_{i,t}, P_{i,t})$, which carries the Individual Treatment Effect, and a
neighbour-aggregated interference term $\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} W_{ij}
\Psi(X_{j,t}, P_{j,t})$, which carries the Spatial Interference Effect. The
split is enforced architecturally: $\Phi$ reads a node-local temporal encoding
that never touches a message-passing operator, so it cannot absorb neighbour
information. We pair the model with two policy-facing indices---a Housing
$+$ Transportation overburden index and a level-aware Displacement Pressure
Index---and evaluate on a controlled synthetic city with a known
data-generating process, across ten random seeds and four policy scenarios.

Our central empirical finding is a boundary condition rather than a blanket
claim. \CGCEN{} attains a pooled MAE of $8.88 \pm 2.40$ against $11.87 \pm
3.12$ for a spatial lag model, $20.03 \pm 7.97$ for a non-graph temporal MLP,
and $27.90 \pm 1.27$ for naive persistence. However, its advantage over the
linear spatial baseline is statistically significant only under active policy
intervention ($p = 0.0015$--$0.0033$ across the three intervention scenarios)
and is \emph{not} significant in the untreated equilibrium ($p = 0.127$).
Topology ablations show the graph is load-bearing: removing edges costs
$+2.89$ MAE and randomly rewiring them costs $+4.88$ MAE, so a wrong topology
is more damaging than no topology at all. We further report a negative result
that shaped the design: under a generator whose spillover is a single permanent
shock, no model---ours included---derives any measurable benefit from the
graph, because permanent early spillover collapses into a static per-node
intercept. Graph learning earns its keep for urban policy only when the
spillover process is genuinely dynamic.
\end{abstract}
